# Marketing — everything in one place

A living document. One file for positioning, channels, budget, ads and results. Update **Focus
this month** on the 1st and move the previous one into the log. Company context lives in
`my-company.md` — that document leads, this one executes it.

**Premise:** roughly 1–2 hours a week for marketing, alongside building. Everything below is
scaled to that. A channel that costs more time than that is not a channel, it is a hobby.

### Note on #3 — pick one industry

The product stays industry-independent. The **marketing** does not. "Planning for SMEs" means
nothing to anyone; "Planning for installation companies with 5–25 engineers" is an
advertisement, a LinkedIn search and an opening line all at once.

Candidates (pick one, the rest come later):

| Industry | For | Against |
|----------|-----|---------|
| Installation / technical services | Lots of SMEs, plans per customer address, used to software | More competition |
| Cleaning | Simple planning, many small companies | Price-sensitive, low willingness to pay |
| Landscaping / gardening | Little software on offer | Smaller, seasonal |
| Trade / construction freelancer collectives | Quick to reach through a network | Often too small, no budget |

**Decision rule:** pick the industry where you already know somebody. A warm contact beats a
perfect market.

## 2. Positioning

**One-liner (draft — rewrite once the industry is chosen):**

> Planning software for [industry] that you understand in an afternoon. No implementation
> project, no consultant, no features you will never get to.

**The wedge:** existing packages are too heavy and too expensive for companies with 5–25
employees.

**Pain points we attack** (validate these in conversations, drop what does not hold):

| Pain | Our promise |
|------|-------------|
| The planning lives in Excel and WhatsApp; nobody knows which version is current | One week overview, always current |
| Somebody scheduled who is not available | Conflict detection while scheduling |
| Existing software: too expensive, too many buttons, weeks to get going | In use the same day |
| Customer data separate from the planning | Customers and planning in one system |

**What we are not** (just as important — it keeps the wrong leads away): time tracking,
invoicing, bookkeeping, inventory, CRM with pipelines, enterprise workflows.

**Proof we are still missing:** screenshots, a demo video, one real customer with a name.
Without proof, every euro spent on advertising is thrown away.

| Disqualifier | >50 employees, an in-house IT department, wants ERP integrations |

## 4. Phasing — what we do when

Do not start a channel from the next phase before this phase's exit criterion is met.

### Phase 0 — Validation (now) · budget €0 · 1 h/week

Manual, one-to-one. The goal is learning, not selling.

- 10 conversations of 20 minutes with companies from the ICP. Ask how they plan today and what
  it costs them when it goes wrong. **Do not pitch.**
- Write down their literal words — that becomes your ad copy.
- **Exit criterion:** 3 companies saying "let me know when it exists" plus an industry chosen.

### Phase 1 — Design partners · budget €0–25/month · 1–2 h/week

- Let 1–3 companies use it free in exchange for feedback and, later, a quote or case study.
- Landing page live: the problem, 3 screenshots, one form. Nothing more.
- **Exit criterion:** 1 company uses it weekly of its own accord.

### Phase 2 — First paying customers · budget €25–75/month · 2 h/week

- Set the price and ask for it. Paying or not is the only real signal.
- A case study from the design partner. Targeted outreach to 20 comparable companies.
- **Exit criterion:** 3 paying customers, and you know which sentence won them over.

### Phase 3 — Making it repeatable · budget €100–300/month · 2 h/week

Only now, ads — see section 6.

- **Exit criterion:** CAC known and lower than six months of revenue per customer.

**Current phase: 0.**

## 6. Ads

**Rule: not one euro on ads until all four are true.**

- [ ] At least 1 paying customer
- [ ] The price is fixed and can be stated out loud
- [ ] The landing page converts visitors into enquiries (at least 3 from organic traffic)
- [ ] You can write down the sentence explaining why that customer said yes

Reason: ads amplify a message, they do not invent one. Without a proven message you are paying
to discover that your page does not work — which you can find out for free.

### When you start (phase 3)

Begin with **Google Search**, not social. Somebody searching "planning software installation
company" already has the problem; somebody scrolling Facebook does not.

| Setting | Starting value |
|---------|----------------|
| Channel | Google Ads, Search only — switch Display off, it is on by default |
| Budget | €5/day, roughly €150/month |
| Match types | Exact and phrase only, no broad |
| Test duration | 6 weeks, then decide |
| Conversion goal | A demo request — not a page view, not a click |
| Stop rule | After €300 without a conversation: off, revise the message |

**Terms to bid on** (fill in after choosing the industry):

software to schedule employees
